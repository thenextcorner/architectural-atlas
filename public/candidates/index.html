39 structures worth modeling next

Research shortlist, October 2026

These are the structures I researched as candidates for future exploded 3D models on Architectural Atlas. Each one is famous enough to earn its place, breaks down into nameable components, has published dimensions, and can be modeled procedurally. The top 15 are ranked by iconicness and modeling fit; the rest of the shortlist follows.

Want one of these built first? Tell me on X at @TheNextCorner. A public vote on what gets built next is coming.

#1EuropeHard

St Peter's Basilica

Vatican City, Vatican City

The world's largest church and seat of the Catholic Church; Michelangelo's dome is the tallest in the world (1506 to 1626).

Key dimensions: 220 m long, 150 m wide; dome 136.6 m high, 42 m outer diameter.

Exploded view would show:

  • double-shell dome (16 ribs + 16 segments)
  • drum with 16 windows
  • lantern and cross
  • 4 massive dome piers
  • nave (46.2 m high)
  • Maderno facade
  • Bernini colonnade (284 columns)
  • St Peter's Square obelisk

Modeling: Double-curvature dome with 16 ribs, layered Renaissance and Baroque detail, and the 284-column colonnade raise the procedural cost.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org

#2EuropeHard

St Paul's Cathedral

London, United Kingdom

Wren's masterpiece (1675 to 1710) and London's skyline icon, famous for surviving the Blitz; the only cathedral with a true triple dome.

Key dimensions: 111 m to the cross; triple dome: inner dome (68 m) + brick cone + outer dome (85 m); 158 m long.

Exploded view would show:

  • inner dome
  • structural brick cone
  • outer lead-covered dome
  • peristyle drum colonnade
  • lantern and cross
  • two 67 m west towers
  • nave and transepts
  • Portland-stone portico

Modeling: The triple-dome stack (three nested shells at different heights) is complex but very legible for an exploded view.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org www.raeng.org.uk

#3North AmericaMedium

Chrysler Building

New York City, United States

The definitive Art Deco skyscraper (1930); held the world height record for 11 months and has the most famous crown in architecture.

Key dimensions: 319 m (1,046 ft), 77 floors; tallest brick building in the world.

Exploded view would show:

  • seven telescoping stainless-steel spire arches
  • eagle gargoyles (61st floor)
  • radiator-cap ornaments (31st floor)
  • setback ziggurat massing
  • brick-and-steel facade grid
  • Art Deco lobby

Modeling: The crown is fiddly geometry, but the tower is a simple extruded massing with repetition-friendly floor tiers.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org www.dezeen.com

#4OceaniaMedium

Sydney Harbour Bridge

Sydney, Australia

The Coathanger: the world's widest long-span bridge (1932) and Sydney's defining structure alongside the Opera House.

Key dimensions: Arch span 503 m; arch top 134 m above sea level; total length 1,149 m; deck 49 m wide.

Exploded view would show:

  • two 28-panel arch trusses
  • deck (8 lanes, 2 rail lines, footway, cycleway)
  • 4 bearing pins
  • hanger cables
  • four 89 m granite pylons
  • skewback foundations

Modeling: The parabolic arch trusses and hanger pattern need careful parametric placement, but everything is geometric steel.

Sources: thisisaustralia.au

#5EuropeHard

St Basil's Cathedral

Moscow, Russia

The nine-onion-domed icon of Russia (1555 to 1561), commissioned by Ivan the Terrible; the most recognizable Russian building on earth.

Key dimensions: About 47.5 m tall; nine domes (eight chapels around a central tenth, added 1588).

Exploded view would show:

  • central tent-roof tower
  • 8 radiating chapel domes
  • 10th sanctuary dome
  • 8 gabled roof chapels
  • brick base structure
  • drum arcades
  • finial crosses

Modeling: Each dome has a unique shape, colour and pattern; lots of bulbous geometry, though still parametric.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org

#6EuropeMedium

Atomium

Brussels, Belgium

The 102 m iron-crystal monument built for Expo 58; Belgium's most iconic structure.

Key dimensions: 102 m tall; nine 18 m-diameter steel spheres; iron unit cell magnified about 165 billion times.

Exploded view would show:

  • nine spheres (six visitable)
  • 20 connecting tubes
  • top sphere and restaurant
  • escalator tubes
  • central vertical tube with elevators
  • three support columns

Modeling: Trivially parametric (spheres and cylinders), but the diagonal tube connections need exact angular placement.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org

#7Southeast AsiaHard

Angkor Wat

Siem Reap, Cambodia

The largest religious monument on earth (early 12th century); its five-tower silhouette is on the Cambodian flag.

Key dimensions: Central spire about 65 m; moat 190 m wide enclosing 1.5 by 1.3 km; about 162.6 ha.

Exploded view would show:

  • central quincunx (5 lotus-bud towers)
  • three concentric galleries
  • cruciform terraces
  • moat and causeways
  • four gopura entrances
  • library annexes
  • bas-relief gallery walls

Modeling: Enormous scope and intricate lotus-bud towers; the symmetrical layout helps, but relief detail is prohibitive at atlas fidelity.

Sources: www.nationalgeographic.com www.livescience.com

#8Southeast AsiaMedium

Petronas Towers

Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia

The world's tallest twin towers (452 m, 1998) joined by a skybridge; the icon of modern Malaysia.

Key dimensions: 452 m, 88 floors; skybridge at 170 m (floors 41 to 42), 750 tonnes; 74 m pinnacles.

Exploded view would show:

  • two identical towers (8-pointed-star plan)
  • double-decker skybridge
  • two 74 m pinnacles
  • entrance lobbies
  • stainless-steel and glass facades
  • base podium and mall

Modeling: Twinning halves the work; the star-plan massing and skybridge are clean geometry.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org

#9East AsiaMedium

Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests

Beijing, China

The triple-gabled circular wooden temple (1420, rebuilt 1889) where Ming and Qing emperors prayed for harvests; a masterpiece of Chinese joinery with no nails.

Key dimensions: 38 m tall, about 32 to 36 m diameter; 28 pillars; three-tier marble base.

Exploded view would show:

  • three concentric blue-tile roofs
  • golden finial ball
  • 28 pillars (4 central + 12 middle + 12 outer)

  • balustrades

Modeling: Circular symmetry and repetition-friendly pillars; the triple-eaved roof is the hardest part.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org

Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

The 38 m Art Deco statue (1931) atop Corcovado, one of the New7Wonders; the most famous statue on the planet.

Key dimensions: 30 m statue + 8 m pedestal = 38 m; 28 m arm span; 635 tonnes.

Exploded view would show:

  • 8 m pedestal

Famous, but not modelable (yet)

  • Great Wall of China Linear and effectively infinite: no natural start or end for an exploded view.
  • Forbidden City, Beijing Sprawling complex: about 980 buildings over 72 ha. It is a campus, not a single decomposable structure.
  • Mount Rushmore, USA Purely sculptural: four portrait heads carved in rock, with no architectural components to explode.
  • Al-Khazneh (Treasury), Petra, Jordan Rock-carved facade: the building is a cliff face, not an assembly of parts.
  • Sistine Chapel, Vatican City Interiors-only: its fame is the ceiling frescoes, which cannot be decomposed into 3D architectural components.
  • Stonehenge, UK Megalithic: only a handful of nameable parts and no documented original full form.
  • Palace of Versailles, France Sprawling complex: palace plus gardens plus Trianons. Too large and diffuse for a single exploded model.

A note on the numbers. Dimensions here come from public web sources gathered during research in October 2026 and were not all verified against primary sources. A few are disputed between sources (the Winter Palace facade length, the Wat Arun prang height, the Potala Palace height). Every figure gets re-verified from authoritative sources before any model is built, and the research files record the disagreements honestly.

Difficulty ratings estimate the procedural modeling effort: Easy means clean parametric geometry, Hard means heavy ornament or complex curved surfaces.
