How this started

It started with a post on X. Someone shared the Human Atlas, an interactive 3D model of the human body that you can take apart piece by piece, and I could not stop playing with it. I kept thinking: this is exactly how I want to look at buildings.

So I built it.

What it is

Architectural Atlas is a fork of the Human Atlas viewer by ashemag, released under the MIT license. The viewer keeps the original idea: orbit a model, pull it apart into an exploded inventory, click any piece to inspect it.

What changed is everything inside. The anatomy dataset is gone. In its place are procedural 3D models of famous buildings, each generated in code from published dimensions. Every building is modeled from scratch as its own program. No two models share geometry.

Each building comes in two versions. Simple gives you the readable silhouette in about 30 parts. Detailed goes further, past 100 named components grouped into systems, with notes on how the pieces fit together. The atlas currently holds 26 buildings and 4,779 named parts, and it keeps growing.

Open by default

All of the modeling code lives in the open, in the GitHub repository: one generator script per building, plus the scripts that validate the models and capture the screenshots. If you read JavaScript, you can see exactly how the Burj Khalifa tapers or how the Golden Gate cables hang.

Why buildings

This is a hobby, and hobbies are allowed to be fun. I have a long list of structures I still want to add, from Gothic cathedrals to modern supertalls, and I build the ones I am most curious about first.
